Privacy Policy

13 November 2026. This page is written in plain language so you can see what happens to information. It is not legal advice, and it does not create a lawyer-client relationship.

Questions about this policy: contact.clayjar@protonmail.com.

The short version

  • Reading and listening are free. No account, no ads, no paywall, and no analytics.
  • Until you sign in, bookmarks, highlights, your reading place, your listening place, and reading settings stay in this browser. They are not sent to a server.
  • Signing in is optional. The sign-in screen says what will sync. If you sign in, that information is copied to your account at Google Firebase and merged with what is already there, so another device can use it.
  • You can save a backup file with no account. Restoring a file merges it into this device. The file is yours. We do not upload it.
  • The sign-in session is stored only in this browser. Signing out asks whether to keep or remove this device’s copy. Deleting the account removes the server copy and this device’s copy.
  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for advertising.
  • The daily devotional email is optional. If you subscribe, your email address is stored with Brevo only to send that devotional. It is never sold, and you can unsubscribe any time.

What stays on your device

With no account, the site stores these in the browser on that device only:

  • Bookmarks and multi-color highlights
  • Reading place
  • Listening place and listening settings (speed, voice choice, whether to continue into the next chapter)
  • Reading settings such as text size, light or dark mode, and whether footnotes are shown

That copy is not uploaded. You can export it as a JSON file from Bookmarks → Backup, and from the Account screen when sign-in is turned on. Importing a file merges it with what is already on the device: existing items are kept, and a newer reading place or setting wins. A backup file you saved is under your control. We cannot delete a file you stored somewhere else.

What syncs if you sign in

Nothing in the list above is sent until you choose to sign in. The sign-in screen states this before you continue. Signing in is the opt-in.

After you sign in, the site stores, in a Google Firebase project, tied to your sign-in:

  • The account identifier Firebase assigns (a uid)
  • The email address, if you use Google or email
  • A phone number only if the owner later turns phone sign-in on and you use it. Phone sign-in is off. It is not part of the free setup, because text messages cost money.
  • Your display name, if the provider sends one
  • Bookmarks, highlights, reading place, listening place, and reading settings

The first sign-in on a device merges what is already on that device with what is already in the account. A deletion on one device is kept, so an older copy does not put it back. The sign-in session itself stays in this browser only (Firebase local persistence). It is not a cookie we share with other sites, and we do not run a separate session server.

Signing out asks you to keep the copy on this device, or to remove bookmarks, highlights, reading place, and listening place from this device. Text size and appearance stay either way. Signing out does not delete the account. “Delete my data and account” removes the Firebase copy and the bookmarks, highlights, and places on this device. That cannot be undone. A backup file you already saved is untouched.

What we do not do

  • No ads, no ad networks, and no sale of personal information.
  • No “sharing” for cross-context behavioral advertising (the CCPA/CPRA sense of share).
  • No analytics and no tracking pixels on this website.
  • We do not require an account to read or listen.
  • We do not sell the devotional list, and we do not use it for ads.

Daily devotional by email

You can ask to receive the day’s devotional by email. This is separate from reading. Opening the site does not sign you up. The form stays hidden until the owner connects it, and it does not load an outside script onto this page.

If you subscribe, you type your email address into the form. The form sends that address to Brevo, the company that keeps the list and sends the message. Brevo then emails you a confirmation link. You are not on the list until you tap that link. The address is stored with Brevo only to send the devotional. It is never sold. It is not used for advertising. You can unsubscribe any time from the link in each email. You can also write to contact.clayjar@protonmail.com and ask to be removed.

The message goes out around 4:00 in the morning, US Eastern time. It contains that day’s title, verse, reflection, and prayer, plus a link back to the devotional page. Bookmarks, highlights, and your reading place are not part of it. Brevo may record that a message was delivered. We do not use that for ads, and we do not sell it.

Who processes information

These are the processors. We do not send them your bookmarks unless you sign in, and we never send them an ad profile because we do not make one.

  • Google Firebase (Authentication and Firestore), only if you sign in. Account data and the synced reading information live there. The project owner chooses the database region when they create it.
  • GitHub Pages hosts this website and the download files of the translation. That is the site itself, not your account. A scheduled job on GitHub sends the day’s devotional through Brevo when the email list is turned on. That job does not receive your address. Brevo does.
  • Brevo stores the email address of a person who subscribes to the daily devotional, and sends that email, including the unsubscribe link. Brevo does not receive bookmarks, highlights, or a reading place. If you never subscribe, Brevo receives nothing about you.
  • Hugging Face hosts the recorded and dramatized audio files of the translation. Those files are the CC BY 4.0 reading, not your listening place. Your listening place stays on the device unless you sign in.

How long it is kept

Information on the device stays until you clear the site’s data in the browser, sign out and choose to remove it, or delete the account (which also clears bookmarks, highlights, and places on that device). Information in Firebase stays until you delete the account or you ask us, at the contact address, to delete it. If you subscribe to the daily devotional, that email address stays at Brevo until you unsubscribe or you ask us, at contact.clayjar@protonmail.com, to remove it. This website does not keep its own copy of that list.

Children

The site is a Bible reader for a general audience. You do not give personal information to read. Do not create an account, and do not subscribe to the devotional email, if you are under 13, or under the age your country requires for this kind of account (in parts of Europe that can be 16). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If we learn an account or a devotional subscription belongs to a child under the age that applies, we will delete it. Write to the contact address if you believe that has happened. This is how we approach the U.S. Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and similar age rules. It is not a statement that every country’s rule is identical.

Laws people ask about

The paragraphs below describe, in ordinary words, how this site lines up with laws readers often ask about. They are not a claim that a regulator has reviewed this page, and they are not legal advice. Where a law gives you a right, you can use the contact address. You can also complain to your own authority. We will not charge you or cut off the free Bible because you asked.

GDPR and UK GDPR

If those laws apply, the lawful basis for the account is the service you asked for by signing in: storing and syncing the reading information listed above (contract). We do not process that information for reading while you are signed out, because it stays on your device. If you subscribe to the devotional email, the lawful basis for that address is your consent. You can withdraw it by unsubscribing, or by writing to the contact address. Withdrawing it does not affect reading the Bible here. You can ask to access information, correct it, delete it, restrict it, or object, and you can take a copy (the backup file is the portable copy of what syncs). You can complain to a supervisory authority. In the UK that is the ICO. In the EU it is the authority in your country.

CCPA / CPRA (California)

We do not sell personal information. We do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. You can ask what we hold, ask us to correct it, and ask us to delete it. You will not be treated differently for asking. The free reader stays free either way.

LGPD (Brazil)

If the LGPD applies, the basis for account data is the sync you requested. You can ask for confirmation, access, correction, anonymization, deletion, portability, and information about who else processes it. You can revoke the choice to sync by signing out and deleting the account.

PIPEDA (Canada)

If PIPEDA applies, we take responsibility for the account information we actually hold, we ask you to sign in before it leaves the device, and you can ask for access and correction at the contact address.

Australia’s Privacy Act

If the Australian Privacy Principles apply, you can ask for access to, and correction of, the account information we hold. You can complain to us first, and you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC).

International transfers

GitHub Pages and Hugging Face may store the public site and the public audio in the United States or other countries. If you sign in, Firebase stores the account in the region the project owner selected, and Google may process it in other countries under Google’s own terms and safeguards. We do not run an extra transfer of your bookmarks beyond that. A backup file moves only where you send it. If you subscribe, Brevo may store the email address in the European Union or another country where Brevo operates, under Brevo’s own terms. That address is still only for the devotional.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and this page is the notice. For a change that affects an account, the sign-in screen will keep stating what syncs. Reading without an account does not depend on accepting a new use of personal information, because that reading still does not send it.
