Privacy Policy

Last updated November 14, 2026. This page is written in plain language so you can see what happens to information. It is not legal advice, and it does not create a lawyer-client relationship.

The person responsible for this website is Jose Santiago, Marshfield, Massachusetts, USA. Questions about this policy: contact.clayjar@protonmail.com. For South Africa’s POPIA, that address is also the information officer. For China’s PIPL and the EU and UK GDPR, that is the contact for the controller.

The short version

  • Reading and listening are free. No account, no ads, no paywall, and no analytics.
  • Opening the site in English does not send the page to Google. If you pick another language, that page is sent to Google Translate. Nothing is sent for translation otherwise.
  • The language you pick is saved only in this browser’s localStorage. It stays on your device.
  • Until you sign in, bookmarks, highlights, your reading place, your listening place, and reading settings stay in this browser. They are not sent to a server.
  • Signing in is optional. If you sign in, that information is copied to your account at Google Firebase.
  • The daily devotional email is optional. If you subscribe, Brevo stores the address only to send that email, and only after you confirm the link (double opt-in). You can unsubscribe any time.
  • A gift is never required. If you choose to donate, including through PayPal, PayPal processes the payment. The gift is not a purchase and is not tax-deductible.
  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for advertising.

What stays on your device

With no account, the site stores these in the browser on that device only:

  • Bookmarks and multi-color highlights
  • Reading place
  • Listening place and listening settings (speed, voice choice, whether to continue into the next chapter)
  • Reading settings such as text size, light or dark mode, and whether footnotes are shown
  • The language you picked, if you picked one, under the localStorage key cjt-lang

That copy is not uploaded. We do not set a cookie to remember the language. You can export bookmarks as a JSON file from Bookmarks → Backup, and from the Account screen when sign-in is turned on. Importing a file merges it with what is already on the device. A backup file you saved is under your control.

Language and Google Translate

The language menu is off until you use it. Loading a page in English does not load a Google script and does not send the page to Google for translation.

If you choose a language other than English, the site opens the current page through Google’s website translator. Google then receives:

  • The page address, including the path, query, and hash (for example the chapter you were reading)
  • The page content Google fetches or that the page sends so the words can be translated
  • Your IP address and ordinary browser data (such as browser type) that Google receives with that request

Google’s own privacy policy is at https://policies.google.com/privacy. Google may process that request in the United States and other countries. We do not control Google’s later use of what Google receives. Choosing a language is the consent for that transfer. Choosing English again, or clearing this site’s data in the browser, stops further translation requests from this device. The saved choice never leaves the device except as part of opening the translated view you asked for.

A machine translation can be wrong. The English Clay Jar Translation on this site is the official text. The copyright and trademark notices stay in English on purpose.

What syncs if you sign in

Nothing in the reading list above is sent until you choose to sign in. The sign-in screen states this before you continue. Signing in is the opt-in.

After you sign in, the site stores, in a Google Firebase project, tied to your sign-in:

  • The account identifier Firebase assigns (a uid)
  • The email address, if you use Google or email
  • A phone number only if phone sign-in is later turned on and you use it. Phone sign-in is off.
  • Your display name, if the provider sends one
  • Bookmarks, highlights, reading place, listening place, and reading settings

The language choice is not part of the synced account. The first sign-in on a device merges what is already on that device with what is already in the account. The sign-in session itself stays in this browser only (Firebase local persistence). It is not a cookie we share with other sites.

Signing out asks you to keep the copy on this device, or to remove bookmarks, highlights, reading place, and listening place from this device. Text size and appearance stay either way. “Delete my data and account” removes the Firebase copy and the bookmarks, highlights, and places on this device.

What we do not do

  • No ads, no ad networks, and no sale of personal information.
  • No “sharing” for cross-context behavioral advertising (the CCPA/CPRA sense of share).
  • No analytics and no tracking pixels on this website.
  • We do not require an account to read or listen.
  • We do not sell the devotional list, and we do not use it for ads.
  • We do not send the page to Google Translate unless you pick a non-English language.

Daily devotional by email

You can ask to receive the day’s devotional by email. This is separate from reading. Opening the site does not sign you up. The form stays hidden until it is connected, and subscribing does not load an outside script onto this page for people who do not use the form.

If you subscribe, you type your email address into the form. The form sends that address to Brevo (the company that keeps the list and sends the message). Brevo then emails you a confirmation link. You are not on the list until you tap that link. That is double opt-in. The address is stored with Brevo only to send the devotional. It is never sold. It is not used for advertising. You can unsubscribe any time from the link in each email. You can also write to contact.clayjar@protonmail.com and ask to be removed.

The message goes out around 4:00 in the morning, US Eastern time. It contains that day’s title, verse, reflection, and prayer, plus a link back to the devotional page. Bookmarks, highlights, and your reading place are not part of it. Brevo may record that a message was delivered. We do not use that for ads, and we do not sell it. Brevo’s privacy policy is at https://www.brevo.com/legal/privacypolicy/.

Who processes information

These are the processors. We do not send them your bookmarks unless you sign in, and we never send them an ad profile because we do not make one.

  • Google Translate, only if you pick a non-English language. Google receives the page address, the page content needed to translate it, and your IP address and browser data. Google’s privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase (Authentication and Firestore), only if you sign in. Account data and the synced reading information live there.
  • GitHub Pages hosts this website and the download files. A scheduled job on GitHub sends the day’s devotional through Brevo when the email list is turned on. That job does not receive your address. Brevo does.
  • Brevo stores the email address of a person who subscribes to the daily devotional, after they confirm, and sends that email, including the unsubscribe link. If you never subscribe, Brevo receives nothing about you.
  • Hugging Face hosts the recorded and dramatized audio files. Those files are the reading, not your listening place. Your listening place stays on the device unless you sign in.
  • PayPal, only if you choose to donate through PayPal. PayPal processes the payment. If you never donate, PayPal receives nothing from this site about you.

How long it is kept

Information on the device stays until you clear the site’s data in the browser, sign out and choose to remove it, or delete the account. The language choice stays until you choose English or clear the site’s data. Information in Firebase stays until you delete the account or you ask us to delete it. A devotional address stays at Brevo until you unsubscribe or you ask us to remove it. A PayPal payment record stays under PayPal’s own retention rules. This website does not keep its own copy of the donation list or the email list.

GDPR and UK GDPR

If those laws apply, Jose Santiago is the controller. The lawful basis for an account is the service you asked for by signing in (contract). The lawful basis for a devotional email address is your consent, given when you subscribe and confirm the link. You can withdraw it by unsubscribing. The lawful basis for sending a page to Google Translate is your consent, given when you pick a language. You withdraw it by choosing English or clearing this site’s data. Withdrawing consent does not affect reading the English text. You can ask to access information, correct it, delete it, restrict it, or object, and you can take a copy. You can complain to a supervisory authority. In the UK that is the ICO. In the EU it is the authority in your country. You can also use the European Data Protection Board’s list of authorities. These rights are not waived by the Terms of Service.

CCPA / CPRA (California)

We do not sell personal information. We do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. Sending a page to Google Translate because you asked for a translation, or sending an email address to Brevo because you subscribed, or sending a payment to PayPal because you donated, is not a sale and is not “sharing” for ads. You can ask what we hold, ask us to correct it, and ask us to delete it. You will not be treated differently for asking. The free reader stays free either way.

LGPD (Brazil)

If the LGPD applies, the basis for account data is the sync you requested. The basis for a devotional address and for a translation request is your consent. You can ask for confirmation, access, correction, anonymization, deletion, portability, and information about who else processes it. You can revoke consent by unsubscribing, choosing English, signing out, and deleting the account.

PIPEDA (Canada)

If PIPEDA applies, we take responsibility for the account, email, and donation information we actually hold. We ask before reading information leaves the device, before an address is stored for the devotional, and before a page is sent to Google Translate. You can ask for access and correction at the contact address. Quebec’s consumer rules are also described in the Terms of Service. They are not waived here.

PIPL (China)

If the Personal Information Protection Law applies, the purpose of a translation request is to show this page in the language you picked. The overseas recipient is Google. The purpose of an account is the sync you asked for. The overseas recipient is Google Firebase. The purpose of a devotional subscription is that email. The overseas recipient may be Brevo. The purpose of a donation is the gift you chose to send. The overseas recipient is PayPal. Picking a language, signing in, subscribing, or donating is a separate act that starts that transfer. You can ask to access, copy, correct, or delete personal information we hold, and you can withdraw consent. Write to the contact address. We do not use personal information for automated decisions that affect your legal rights.

APPI (Japan)

If the Act on the Protection of Personal Information applies, a transfer to Google, Brevo, PayPal, GitHub, or Hugging Face is a transfer to a third party in a foreign country. Those companies publish their own privacy policies. The countries where they process data may not have a regime identical to Japan’s. You start a transfer only by signing in, subscribing, donating, or picking a language. You can ask for disclosure, correction, and deletion at the contact address.

If the Australian Privacy Principles apply, you can ask for access to, and correction of, the information we hold. You can complain to us first, and you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC). Overseas recipients are named in the processor list above.
